Food that supplies your species' γ nutrient.

What it meansThis food belongs to the γ nutrient list for your dinosaur.

The three-way nutrient glyph, associated with combined-diet food such as sanctuary mushrooms.

What it meansThe detected food can contribute across all three nutrient types instead of representing a single α, β or γ source.

01Your first life: the first 30 minutesWhat to do from spawn instead of wandering until something kills you.

Before moving

  1. Open Tab and look at your species, growth, hunger, thirst, nutrients and any active status effects.
  2. Tap/hold Q to orient with the compass and scent information.
  3. Open the Primeval Refuge Map on a second screen or browser tab and press Center on me. Learn the name of your starting area.
  4. Pick one immediate objective: water if thirsty, food if hungry, or cover if both are comfortable.

Your first travel rule

Do not sprint everywhere. Travel with enough stamina left to escape. Move through cover, pause before crossing open terrain, and approach water from vegetation instead of walking directly down an exposed bank.

First-life priority order

1. Stay alive2. Water3. Food4. Nutrients5. Growth6. Social / combat

What new players usually do wrong

  • Following every roar because it sounds interesting.
  • Draining stamina before reaching water or a risky crossing.
  • Standing still on a riverbank while drinking.
  • Chasing a perfect diet through dangerous territory while already starving.
  • Testing combat on adults while still juvenile.
02Reading the HUD and Status ReportUnderstand what the game is telling you before a warning becomes a death.

Tab is your detailed dashboard

Use the character/status screen to inspect your current condition, diet, preferred foods, growth, mutations and other lifecycle information. The exact layout moves between updates, but the information hierarchy stays similar.

HungerYour food reserve. At zero, survival and growth collapse quickly.
ThirstYour hydration reserve. Plan water stops before it becomes critical.
StaminaYour escape, combat and ability reserve. Do not treat 0 stamina as a minor inconvenience.
GrowthYour maturity progress. Larger growth generally means stronger stats and more unlocked systems.
Nutrientsβ / γ / α diet values that affect growth speed and regeneration bonuses.
Heartbeat / status colourA quick overall warning system. Yellow/orange means manage the problem; red means disengage and fix it immediately.

Wounded: community references report a wounded state around the lower-health range, with reduced combat effectiveness. If your UI is showing a serious injury state, stop playing as though you are healthy.

04Scent, compass and learning GatewayYour nose is the closest thing Evrima gives you to a survival radar.

How to use scent properly

  • Use scent before committing to a route, not only after you are already starving.
  • Compare the direction of water/food/zone cues with the Primeval Refuge Map instead of following a marker blindly.
  • Gore icons are information about player activity as much as they are food information.
  • At night or in thick vegetation, sound + scent + compass matter more than eyesight.

Learning the map without memorising it

  1. Center the live map on your dinosaur.
  2. Read the current area name.
  3. Pick one landmark: river bend, lake, road, coast, ridge or structure.
  4. Walk to the next landmark while checking compass direction.
  5. Repeat. After a few sessions, the named regions will start making sense naturally.

Simple rule: if an icon is useful to you, it is probably useful to another player too. Expect traffic around water, migrations, corpses and popular landmarks.

05Food, diet and the α / β / γ systemWhy filling your stomach is not the same thing as eating well.

Your stomach keeps you alive. Your diet determines how efficiently your dinosaur grows and recovers. Preferred foods contribute one or more nutrient symbols.

β ProteinHealth regeneration bonus; contributes up to 100% growth rate.
γ LipidsFracture regeneration bonus; contributes up to 100% growth rate.
α CarbohydratesBleed regeneration bonus; contributes up to 100% growth rate.
β + αUp to 200% growth plus bleed-resistance-oriented combo benefits.
β + γUp to 200% growth plus fracture-resistance-oriented combo benefits.
α + γUp to 200% growth plus locked-health recovery-oriented combo benefits.
β + γ + αUp to 300% growth and the strongest combined-diet package, including stamina-regeneration benefits.

Practical diet strategy

  • If you are starving, eat safe food first. Do not die trying to optimise a symbol.
  • If stable, deliberately fill missing nutrient types instead of repeatedly topping up one.
  • Herbivore preferred foods can be strongly tied to migration/zone systems.
  • Carnivores should treat carcasses as contested resources; feed while listening and keep an exit route.
  • Salt licks can help clear an unwanted nutrient more quickly when used through the diet/status interface.

Balance warning: exact percentages and food assignments can change between patches. Use the current in-game preferred-food panel as the final authority for your species.

06Water, stamina, resting and safe loggingMost avoidable deaths happen while travelling, drinking or trying to escape with nothing left in the tank.

Drinking safely

  • Stop in cover before the bank and listen.
  • Choose a point with an escape route; do not trap yourself against a cliff or deep channel.
  • Drink, then move away from the shoreline instead of standing there checking menus.
  • Remember that aquatic predators can use water as cover.

Stamina discipline

Keep an emergency reserve. Sprinting everywhere makes travel feel faster until you meet a predator with full stamina. Slower movement also usually turns more tightly, which matters in combat.

Rest and logout

H rests. Holding H enters the sleep/safe-log flow on common current defaults. Hide first. Do not begin a logout ritual in the middle of a known traffic route.

07Migration, Patrol and Sanctuary zonesWhat the zone symbols are trying to make you do — and why they can also be dangerous.
MigrationLarge movement/resource objectives. Especially important to herbivores because preferred plant availability and yield can be tied to migrations. Carnivores often follow the traffic.
PatrolSmaller areas that guide local activity. They can help you find resources or players and are part of some lifecycle progression systems.
SanctuaryJuvenile-focused areas. Young herbivores can use sanctuary mushrooms for combined nutrients. Sanctuaries are not permanent safe zones; larger dinosaurs are eventually pushed out, including through bee pressure.
Mass Migration (MMZ)A larger migration-scale event/zone concept used in progression and Prime conditions. Treat it as high-traffic content rather than a peaceful gathering.

Primeval Refuge map note: our Migration and Patrol overlays are reference geometry. The current RCON feed does not expose authoritative live zone activation, so the website will not pretend a reference zone is LIVE when it cannot prove it.

08Growth and life stagesGetting bigger changes your strength, your risks and what systems become available.

Evrima progression is a survival clock. You mature through young stages toward adulthood, gaining size, stats and access to more lifecycle systems. Exact stage thresholds and species curves are balance-sensitive, so your current growth percentage is more reliable than memorising old patch charts.

What speeds growth up

  • Keeping hunger and thirst from reaching zero.
  • Maintaining useful diet nutrients — one, two or three filled types can raise the growth-rate ceiling substantially.
  • Using migration/sanctuary resources where your species benefits from them.

What changes as you grow

  • You become harder to hide and require more resources.
  • Your combat matchups change; something you could outrun at 15% may become a different threat at 70%.
  • Mutation/lifecycle slots and nesting eligibility become relevant as you mature.
  • Routes change. A tiny juvenile can use dense cover and gaps that an adult cannot.
09Staying alive: reading danger before combatThe best fight is often the one you notice early enough to avoid.

Use your ears

Footsteps, splashes, calls, feeding sounds and vegetation movement can warn you before you see anything. Headphones are a survival tool.

High-risk locations

  • Exposed riverbanks and popular water crossings.
  • Fresh corpses or multiple gore icons.
  • Migration/patrol activity.
  • Open fields with limited cover.
  • Narrow valleys or cliffs with only one escape route.

Before entering a risky space

  1. Stop and listen.
  2. Check stamina.
  3. Identify where you will run if attacked.
  4. Look behind you before crossing.
  5. Cross decisively; do not stop in the middle to inspect the map.
10Combat basics without the panicPosition, stamina and knowing your species matter more than clicking faster.

The universal rules

  • LMB / RMB are your primary/secondary attacks; Alt combinations often access a species-specific alternate move.
  • Do not start a fight on empty stamina.
  • Sprinting gives speed but usually commits you to wider turns; controlled movement can out-turn a faster opponent.
  • Know which end of the enemy is dangerous. Herbivores may be strongest behind them; large carnivores may dominate head-on.
  • Do not chase into unknown terrain just because the opponent is low.

Pounce / grapple

Several species can latch or grapple. If you are the attacker, stamina is your clock. If you are the defender, bucking/clearing effects costs stamina too. Terrain matters: cliffs, water and obstacles can turn a successful latch into a death.

Fight-or-flight check

Do I know the species?Do I have stamina?Can I escape?Is there a second enemy?Am I already bleeding?Do I actually need this fight?
11Damage, bleeding, fractures and status effectsDifferent problems need different responses — HP is only one part of surviving a fight.
Raw damageDirect health loss from attacks. Disengage if visual/status damage becomes severe.
BleedDamage-over-time that is worsened by movement. Break line of sight, stop sprinting and rest somewhere safe. Bleeding can kill you even after the attacker leaves.
FractureReduces mobility and can lock health until it heals. Time, safety and rest matter more than immediately re-entering combat.
BacteriaAssociated especially with Ceratosaurus-style bacterial pressure and bad-food interactions. High buildup can force vomiting and waste your resources; salt is an important counter mechanic in current community references.
VenomStaged damage/status pressure used by species such as Troodon. Escaping the attacker does not instantly erase the effect, so create distance and survive the timer.
Blindness / spitVision impairment from species abilities. Clear the effect using the appropriate interaction/buck input on current defaults and move into cover rather than fighting blind in open ground.

Bleed lesson: one of the most common beginner deaths is sprinting away successfully and then bleeding out because you never stopped to recover.

12Mutations: what the hexagons are forPassive traits that shape how this individual dinosaur plays.

Mutations are passive abilities equipped into lifecycle slots. They can improve hydration, stamina, vision, injury resistance, digestion, reproduction and many other systems.

Three useful categories to understand

  • Lifecycle mutations: normal choices that become available as you mature.
  • Slot-specific mutations: options that can only appear in particular mutation slots.
  • Unlockable lifestyle mutations: become available after completing certain behaviours during the life, such as sustained diet conditions or movement tasks.

Inherited mutations

Nesting can pass inherited traits to offspring through smaller inheritance slots. These are separate from your normal lifecycle choices.

Beginner selection rule

Choose traits that fix a problem you actually have. If you constantly run dry on stamina, a stamina-oriented build is more valuable to you than a niche combat mutation you never use.

Patch-sensitive system: mutation pools, values and unlock conditions change. Read the in-game description before confirming a choice.

13Groups, friends and spawn codesHow to meet people without turning every friendly call into a death sentence.

Same-species groups improve awareness and can make travel safer, but grouping also makes more noise and requires more food.

  • Friendly calls can help communicate/group, but calls reveal your location.
  • Spawn codes can be used to bring friends into the same area/species flow where the current build supports it; invitations are handled through the group/status interfaces.
  • Do not blindly trust strangers because they used a friendly call. Stay positioned so you can leave.
  • Large groups create food pressure. A safe area for one juvenile may not support six.
14Nesting and raising youngThe full loop: partner, nest, eggs, incubation and keeping the babies alive.

Nesting is an advanced survival loop. Do it once you can reliably feed, drink and defend yourself.

  1. Pair: eligible same-species adults/subadults court; current common defaults use N for courting functions.
  2. Place/build: choose defensible terrain and build the nest; mound nests commonly use B.
  3. Gather: sniff for nest material, carry with G, deposit/interact with E.
  4. Gestate: the female prepares eggs; good resources make the process much easier to survive.
  5. Incubate: protect and maintain the nest until players hatch in.
  6. Raise: young still need food, water and protection. A successful nest creates more resource demand immediately.

Bad nest locations: open fields, obvious shorelines, popular migration routes and anywhere you cannot feed/drink without exposing the entire family.

15Prime and Elder — advanced lifecycle goalsWhat the late-game progression terms mean once ordinary survival makes sense.

Prime is an advanced lifecycle progression state earned by completing a set of life objectives before later maturity. Community references currently describe objectives involving sanctuary visits, nesting, balanced diet, migration/patrol participation and raising offspring. Elder is later-life progression layered on top of adulthood, with Prime status affecting the stronger Prime Elder route.

As a beginner, you do not need to optimise Prime on your first few lives. Learn to survive, navigate, eat properly and understand your species first. Once you can reach later growth consistently, Prime objectives give you a reason to experience more of Evrima's systems.

Highly patch-sensitive: thresholds, condition counts and exact Elder/Prime behaviour have changed during recent development. Use the current in-game lifecycle/mutation UI for exact requirements on the live build.
